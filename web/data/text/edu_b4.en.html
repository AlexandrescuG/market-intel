CHAPTER 4 — THE CASE OF THE FIVE POINTS

The Case of the Five Points

The last episode ended on a mystery: the stop sat at 2494, but filled at 2489. The broker didn't cheat you. The market wasn't "hunting" you. It's just that at 14:30:03, at the 2494 level, there wasn't a single buyer — and your order went down the queue, sweeping up whoever was there: 2492… 2490… 2489. This chapter is about the queue. After it, you won't see candles anymore — you'll see what they're made of.

ANTI-MYTH

"On an exchange you can always buy and sell at the price on screen"

The price on screen is the price of the LAST trade — the past. Your trade fills against the CURRENT order queue — and between those two numbers lies everything: spread, depth, slippage. In a quiet hour the difference is pennies, and the myth is harmless. At 14:30 on NFP Friday the difference is points and tens of points, and the myth costs money. A professional isn't different from a novice because of forecasts — they know AT WHAT PRICE their order will actually fill.

SBF'S VOICE

"Colleague, novices study 'where the price will go.' That's half the craft. The other half is 'how I get in and how I get out': what order type, into what liquidity, at what spread. I've seen strategies with beautiful statistics that died from one thing — execution. Five points of slippage on a trade targeting fifteen is minus a third of the profit, before any analysis. This chapter is boring, like an engine room. But that's exactly where it gets decided whether the ship makes it."

THE PRICE OF NOT KNOWING

"Spreads from 0.1 points"

A trader trades with a broker advertising "spreads from 0.1 points." They make 20 trades a week with market orders, half of them in the first minutes after news releases, when the spread widens 10-30x. At an average overpay of just 1.5 points per trade, that's minus 30 points a week — minus 120 a month. Their strategy, targeting +150 points a month, was profitable. Their execution wasn't. They left the market convinced "the strategy stopped working." The strategy worked. They were paying the queue without knowing it.

MAIN INTERACTIVE

A Candle Under the Microscope

This is the order book (DOM, Depth of Market) — the queue of everyone who wants to buy or sell right now. Top right — sellers (ASK): these are the prices at which you can BUY. Bottom left — buyers (BID): these are the prices at which you can SELL. The gap between the best BID and best ASK is the spread: it's not a broker commission, it's the price of immediacy — what you pay for not wanting to wait. The numbers next to the prices are order volume: how many lots are queued at each level. The tape below is the log of completed trades: price, volume, second. The chart shows the past; the book shows the present; the tape shows the exact moment of exchange.

TOOLS OF THE QUEUE

Order Types

An order type is your answer to one question: what matters more to you — PRICE or GUARANTEED ENTRY? Market says: "get me in now, price is secondary." Limit: "only at my price, I might not get in at all." Stop: "get me in once the market proves the move — even at a worse price." Stop-Limit: "prove the move, but I won't pay more than this." Everything else is a combination of these four answers.

Waiting for a pullback to support at 2480

BUY LIMIT at 2480: the queue works for me, I don't pay the spread.

BUY STOP above the level: I pay for confirmation of the move.

News in 3 minutes, I want to be in the market

I stop myself: a market order into an empty queue is the most expensive way to learn about spread.

Limit orders only, and don't be surprised by partial fills.

SBF's rule: a market order is a tool for the liquid hour. The rest of the time, the queue should work for you, not the other way around.

Drag the line above or below the current price — the label updates itself.

You placed the order above price. You can only buy above the market on a breakout — so that's BUY STOP. You can only sell above the market at a limit — so that's SELL LIMIT.

You placed the order below price. You can only buy below the market at a limit — so that's BUY LIMIT. You can only sell below the market on a breakout — so that's SELL STOP.

The price crawls across the chart. Your job is to execute six order types correctly: place the line where that type actually makes sense (BUY LIMIT — below price, BUY STOP — above, and the reverse for SELL; STOP LIMIT and TRAILING STOP follow their own logic). Place it wrong, and the trainer will explain why the order won't fire, or will fire differently than you expected.
